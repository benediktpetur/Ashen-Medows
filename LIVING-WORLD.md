# The Waterline: living-world update

Ashen Meadow's survivor layer revolves around names, supplies and promises. Communities are places with stores and needs, not unlimited reward dispensers. Changes are persistent per world seed; character health, research and gear remain portable.

## Just a Fever / Noah and Lily

The initial meeting offers help with one disclosure (your name, solitary travel, or an existing camp location), or refusal. Refusal costs nothing and changes no community reputation. Walking away from the house leaves the bracing intact and does not start the accusation arc.

Inside the house, Lily holds a worn teddy with a blue ribbon. Examining her reveals the bite without automatically breaking the door. Scratches, the feeding slit, moaning and dialogue foreshadow the mother having turned.

- Open: adult infected combat begins; Noah escapes with Lily even if the player abandons the fight.
- Reinforce: spends 8 timber and 6 fiber, buys 20 active minutes, then Noah leaves with the increasingly ill Lily. Their mother remains behind the intact bracing.
- Confront and tell the truth: opens the real adult fight. When the mother dies, Noah rejects the player and takes Lily into the woods.
- Leave: retains a closed door and a persistent, distinct journal outcome without a reputation penalty.

Flight chooses and saves a grave site in an unvisited woodland cell. After five active minutes the grave, teddy, coat, abandoned shelter and Noah's notebook can be discovered. The note describes his staying with Lily until her death, burying her and leaving alone. The bear remains at her grave. Children are visual/story actors rather than combat targets.

Mercy Row is far away in the city. On arrival after the woodland aftermath, Noah identifies the player. Ada the medic refuses to expel them; Rook the warden closes services. Noah uses the particular disclosure: the name goes on a denied-entry list, solitary travel becomes leverage for expulsion, or the camp coordinates expose up to two food portions in nearby open storage. The group's services remain closed even if ordinary donations rebuild trust.

There is one partial-redemption route: restore the clinic with 8 clean water, 3 medical kits, 12 alloy and 6 electronics within one active day (60 minutes) after the confrontation. Restores services, permanently repairs clinic/water infrastructure, and leaves Noah unwilling to forgive or join the player's camp. Theft or violence closes this route. Medical kits can be crafted on the appropriate research path or bartered from friendly communities for food and alloy.

If the city is never reached within six active days (six real play hours) after flight, the failed evacuation and final ledger can be found at abandoned Mercy Row, with a familiar coat beside a grave. Online world time continues in menus; solo pause and time offline do not advance these clocks. Completed old versions of the quest remain completed; unstarted/incomplete pre-breach saves migrate to the new choices.

## Neighboring communities

Emberstead (woodland growers), Switchyard (highway mechanics), and Mercy Row (city medics) have named residents, shelters, shared food/clean-water stores, trust, services, visible activity and perimeter guards. Map markers and F7 -> The Waterline ledger expose their locations and remembered outcomes.

Donating food/water transfers actual inventory. Repairing a communal filter permanently stops its water depletion. Services consume the community's own stores and have replenishment cooldowns. Theft transfers food out of its ration store, lowers trust and morale, and can make guards hostile. Killing a guard permanently evacuates the camp and removes its services. Guards defend against ordinary infected without awarding the player passive kill loot/XP. Communities begin supply simulation when first met; prolonged empty stores can force evacuation. Unmet settlements are not silently exhausted at the start of a new world.

Existing roadside encounter decisions now also enter the world ledger and affect personal morale; nearby communities already met can hear about supplies or medical help. Their existing rewards, saved choices and guardian consequences still apply. This is a connected consequence system, not a claim that every older vignette has a six-stage story arc.

## Research identity

Each character can earn 48 research points in total across worlds. Awards are deduplicated and cannot farm around the cap by changing seeds. Workbenches, basic materials/ammo, bandages and foundational shelter stay available through existing progression.

The first paid advanced purchase commits a specialization in that tab. T4/T5 plans need an earlier advanced plan in that same family, plus normal workshop, material and skill gates. Other advanced families in that tab close permanently; no free research reset. Existing learned blueprints are retained when loading older characters.

- Weapons/armor: Ranger, Brawler, Ballistics.
- Buildings: Homesteader, Engineer, Sanctuary.
- Defenses: Fortifier, Sentinel, Trapper.
- Survival: Waterworks, Herbalist, Pathfinder.

New crafted survival equipment: charcoal filters (resin per batch), membrane purifiers (fuel instead), sealed vessels (four water per batch rather than two), comfort kits (sheltered recovery near owned sleeping bags), antibiotic procedures (double crafted medical doses), and trail tea kits (25% less ongoing fatigue loss). Choices restrict crafting knowledge; finding or trading for equipment remains useful.

## Health, death and water

Health is a visible bar. Untreated wounds drain 4.5 HP per minute each, capped at 21.6 HP/minute; old stored blood counters never cause automatic death. Bandaging immediately stops that wound's bleed without restoring HP; continuous bandaging still heals the wound over 20 active minutes. Wounds, illness and death trauma lower the natural-recovery ceiling, shown as an amber marker. Slow recovery starts after 30 seconds without impact damage, adequate food/water and no open bleeding.

Death retains earned levels, research, specialization and gear; current-level XP is still lost. Respawn starts at 70% health, 70 needs, loses one carried food portion, lowers morale, and causes ten active minutes of recovery trauma. Sickness and open wounds clear so a respawn is playable.

Wells produce untreated water. Each untreated drink has a saved deterministic 35% sickness roll, rather than guaranteed poisoning. Bottled, boiled and filtered water is clean and has no illness risk. Basic boiling requires a workbench and two timber. Purification conserves the quantity of water; storage accepts clean water separately from the untreated inventory. Y provides treatment and water controls.

Sickness slows movement and stamina recovery and reduces healing; fatigue, low morale, hunger and thirst add their own restrictions. High morale, good nutrition and safe rest provide explicit benefits. These values persist and are synchronized per character. Medical kits cure water illness and support gradual healing; they do not instant-fill the health bar.

Multiplayer protocol is 25. All players need this update. World records/settlements are host-owned; conditions, water, supplies and research are validated and synchronized for each player independently.

## A dog and the people who remember

There is one rescue dog per world/seed, near the southeast starting trail. Approach and press F; gaining its trust costs one food. It has a clean animal model and blue collar, separate from the infected hounds. No scripted death, replacement, resurrection, or combat catch-up teleport. An unrescued dog stays at its encounter; after rescue, normal nearby zombies can target it. Packs, stronger enemies, special attacks, Foreman attacks, acid and exploding cars can kill it permanently. Its body/name remain saved at the death location. Player death does not reset its fate.

The dog has 100 health and a 22 damage bite every 1.8 seconds. It is strong against a lone tier-one walker; tier-two walkers are a dangerous, roughly even fight. It does not scale into an invincible boss companion. Early curiosity engages enemies up to 13m away; trained dogs limit this to 7m. Dormant crying infected and passive story enemies are not deliberately provoked.

Naming unlocks after 15 active minutes together and bond 15. Enter a name of up to 24 characters. Bond grows by spending time together and caring for it. Sneaking together develops stealth from 0–100: barking attracts enemies over 26m initially, falling to 3m, then stops at stealth 90. Barking is real world noise, including while holding position. Early noise is intentional risk. Care costs one food, restores 15 health and adds 3 bond, with a five-minute active cooldown. It slowly recovers near its owner while not fighting; it does not lose health merely because time passes offline.

F7 → Your dog shows its state. Follow defends and engages; Stay holds its position and defends only at close range; Recall disengages and runs toward the owner, without attacking. Commands work within 100m; rescue, naming and care require standing within 3.5m. Only its rescuer can give orders. It waits outside dungeons. No teleport rescues it from a fight. Watch its health and use recall before it gets surrounded.

Reputation is saved per player identity, per world. Each discovered settlement has a personal standing for that player, and each of its four named residents has a separate standing/memory. Your own camp and its rescued residents also have standing. F7 → People / Personal reputation displays them. Shared settlement trust still represents the community's world state: theft or violence can close a settlement for everyone, while personal standing records who acted.

Donation/repair/theft changes the acting player's settlement and resident relationships. Individual meal sharing costs one food and improves only that person; ten-minute cooldowns prevent gift spam. Trusted residents may offer a ration from actual settlement stores. No personal favor bypasses Mercy Row's story restrictions. Camp rescue, recruitment, aid and real supply deposits improve relationships; injuries while following a player damage that relationship, and a distrustful resident refuses to follow. Empty deposits give no standing. Scores run from -100 to 100: Distrusted, Wary, Acquaintance, Trusted, Close. The dog's bond is its companion relationship, rather than a human trade score.

Multiplayer protocol 26: the host owns dog simulation, permanent fate and relationships; guests receive world snapshots, and their food, commands and reputation are validated against their own identity. Update everyone together.

## Settlement storylines and the Black Ledger

Four settlements now exist. The Black Ledger occupies a fortified city lot south of the river, with Voss, Mercer, Rhea and Kane plus its guard. Emberstead has crop beds, Switchyard has workshop benches/salvage machinery, Mercy Row has clinic beds and medical markings, and the Ledger has metal walls and red standards. Successful development visibly adds market tables, project structures, defenses or covered facilities, and stores. Lost residents leave named empty places and grave markers; an exterminated settlement is permanently closed.

Approach a settlement and press F, then choose its story or developed trade. F7 → The Waterline also opens discovered story pages, but completing choices and trading requires returning to the settlement. The HUD tracks the soonest deadline and retaliation; M marks missing-person sites, the seized shipment and a known raid destination.

Each friendly settlement has three chapters:

* **The Last Harvest / Emberstead:** Tessa is missing on a seed-supply run. After rescuing or abandoning her, choose Iris's civilian lifeline or Jules's defense line. Finally choose a mutual-aid network, an exclusive arsenal, or taking the project's last stock as payment.
* **What the Yard Owes / Switchyard:** Rafi's recovery truck has not returned. Gus's parts and Nia's concern for the driver lead into a recovery bay versus reinforced workshop decision, followed by shared relief or an exclusive weapons economy.
* **The Beds We Keep / Mercy Row:** Eli is trapped with the clinic's dressings. Saving him supports Ada's surgery project; Rook favors a defended quarantine perimeter. Their future relief/arsenal decision is separate from Noah's clinic crisis: no new trade or favor bypasses that crisis.

The first visit starts the missing-person deadline: 30 active minutes, visibly announced. Find the marked site, defeat its real infected guardians and spend one bandage. Ignoring it or choosing abandonment kills the named scout permanently. A successful rescue grants one development level. Chapters two and three each have a one-active-hour deadline and one irreversible decision; missing rebuilding causes a permanent decline ending. Every successful chapter can add development, to a maximum of three. Losing the scout means the normal route cannot reach the best stock. Supply donations cannot resurrect residents or replay failed chapters.

Civilian rebuilding costs 16 timber, 4 clean water, 4/8/12 alloy and 0/1/2 electronics by settlement. Fortification costs 24 timber, 10/15/20 alloy and 0/1/2 electronics. The final mutual-aid choice costs 6 food and 4 water, adds stores and sends supplies to another surviving friendly community. The exclusive arsenal costs 8/12/16 alloy and 2/3/4 electronics. Cashing out gives immediate alloy/food, reduces development and costs 20 personal trust.

Trade develops from supply packs to settlement gear, then specialist weapons or civilian relief reserves. Stock is finite and replenishes every 15 active minutes. Your personal reputation, the community's shared trust, its development, and permanent policy all matter. Destroyed communities never restock. Weapon purchases require discovering their material tier and meeting the existing equip gate; the legendary-heart upgrade remains a boss reward. Purchased items go to the acting player's saved inventory, not the host's.

### The extermination order

Meeting the Ledger and leaving is safe. Accepting its first contract requires tier-four discovery and explicitly puts your camp at risk. Recover the physically guarded seized shipment within 30 active minutes; its manifest identifies confiscated survivor supplies. Returning it opens the first city stock and starts a 60-active-minute ultimatum for the next order. Ignoring, refusing after commitment, or failing a contracted deadline makes the Ledger hostile and schedules one armed retaliation.

Voss targets the first surviving early settlement: Emberstead, otherwise Switchyard. At acceptance, your personal standing with that target locks the route. At 20 or higher you infiltrate using their trust; below 20 you assault. Both routes permanently kill four adult residents and their guard, close the victim's market, and unlock the Ledger's best city stock. The other friendly communities and your own residents lose trust; a distrustful human companion may refuse to keep following.

Assault uses physical human defenders with melee weapons and firearms, ordinary hit reactions and health. Infiltration requires interacting with separate water, gate-radio and fuel-store objects inside the settlement. Disable water and radio, then spend four fuel to ignite the stores. The residents discover the betrayal and resist; fire damages residents who remain inside. Living targets must actually die for the order to complete. The objective does not grant rewards just for pressing an execution button. Breaking cover by killing their guard before the attack instead evacuates the settlement and fails the order.

Before ignition, you can expose the order at the target settlement and spend 8 food, 8 clean water, 20 timber, 12 alloy and 2 electronics on its protection. Its surviving residents keep their home, it becomes a mature mutual-aid ally, and you lose the Ledger's market. The retaliation comes to your own camp. This is a costly alternative, not a secret way to receive both factions' rewards.

### Retaliation

There are three active minutes of warning. The destination is an existing camp, sleeping bag or built site; if you have no home yet, retaliation waits for one. It spawns four armed human raiders when players occupy the area. They can shoot the player, attack nearby recruited survivors and the dog, and damage actual placed structures. Existing turrets, traps, companions and combat damage work against them. Killing all four ends the finite raid; dead raiders do not respawn. If the occupied camp is overrun for five active minutes, remaining raiders withdraw after stealing real food/water stores and leaving nearby residents injured and demoralized. Building damage remains. No camp is silently destroyed while everyone is offline.

All branch state, lost residents, development, market stock, sabotage, faction hostility and raid outcomes are saved in the host world. Multiplayer protocol 27: update every player together. The host validates nearby actions; quest resources and purchased gear belong to the player who acted.

Raid resolution also checks for a real breach: a surviving raider must reach within 12m of the camp center with an unobstructed route before the raid can steal stores. If the perimeter holds for the assault's five active minutes, surviving attackers withdraw without stealing supplies. Injuries from an overrun apply only to residents near the attacked camp. Warning a threatened settlement also supplies its stores, flushes the sabotaged water and restores its filter.
