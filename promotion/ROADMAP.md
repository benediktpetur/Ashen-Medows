# Ashen Meadow — proposed development roadmap

Draft direction · 16 October 2026 · Ordered by readiness, without promised dates

**The destination:** a survival expedition game where preparing at camp, venturing into danger, and returning with something valuable form one satisfying journey.

## Foundation — in the current development build

- First-person exploration and combat across meadow, woodland, outskirts, city, and underground areas.
- Tiered workshops, crafting, equipment, and six activity-trained skills.
- Hunger, thirst, fatigue, persistent wounds, and gradual recovery.
- Structural building, tree felling, paths, wells, sleeping bags, and saved world changes.
- Seven dungeon entrances, guarded loot, night threats, wildlife, and recovered story fragments.
- Host-led direct-connect co-op with eight slots, independent surface exploration, and party dungeon travel.
- A Windows launcher and release workflow prepared for playtest distribution.

These systems exist; their presence does not imply final balance, final artwork, or completed large-group internet validation.

## Next — make the expedition dependable

**Player outcome:** understand how to begin, trust progress to save, and enjoy a complete early expedition alone or with friends.

- Test the route from the trailhead to the first meaningful upgrade and dungeon return.
- Clarify survival feedback, crafting requirements, gear comparisons, and wound treatment.
- Tune early supplies, enemy pressure, recovery, and the pace of equipment upgrades.
- Run real internet co-op sessions, including reconnects, separated explorers, latency, and larger groups.
- Profile longer sessions and crowded areas; address stability and performance findings.

**Ready to move on when:** new testers can complete the early loop without developer guidance, critical progress-loss defects are resolved, and group tests establish reliable supported play conditions.

## Then — give every expedition a purpose

**Player outcome:** choose a destination for a reason and return with a discovery that changes the next trip.

- Strengthen the rewards and identity of each progression region.
- Refine dungeon encounters, navigation, environmental storytelling, and reward pacing.
- Give night expeditions a clearer balance of danger and opportunity.
- Improve the value of camps and expedition preparation; evaluate building costs and shared-resource fairness through playtests.
- Extend progression only where existing tiers leave meaningful gaps.

**Ready to move on when:** testers can explain why they chose a route, what they gained, and what they want to attempt next; solo and co-op progression both remain rewarding.

## Later — bring the world into focus

**Player outcome:** recognize Ashen Meadow from its landscapes, characters, sound, and atmosphere.

- Develop the graphic-novel visual direction with clearer silhouettes and more cohesive environments.
- Improve character movement, weapon handling, enemy animation, and impact feedback.
- Add richer ambient audio and more distinctive location dressing.
- Refine settings, accessibility, and onboarding using observed player needs.
- Prepare a representative gameplay trailer and a clearer public playtest presentation.

**Ready to move on when:** representative gameplay communicates the intended identity, important interactions read clearly, and the presentation performs acceptably on tested target hardware.

## Horizon — ideas to explore

These are possible expansions, subject to prototypes, scope, and player feedback:

| Idea | What it could add | What must be proven first |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Survivor communities | Relationships, trade, and reasons to revisit places | NPC behavior and interactions deepen the expedition loop |
| Boss encounters | Memorable challenges and distinctive rewards | Combat supports readable, fair, repeatable encounters |
| Drivable vehicles | Longer journeys and new transport choices | Terrain, networking, and world scale support useful driving |
| Beyond the river | New places, materials, threats, and stories | Existing regions already feel worth exploring |
| Easier online access | Less friction when playing with friends | Hosting approach, operating costs, and support scope are established |

## How priorities will be chosen

Fix lost progress and broken sessions first. Improve the early journey next. Add content when it creates a meaningful new decision. Expand the world when the existing world gives players a reason to stay.

Update the public roadmap after substantive playtest findings. Move features into “current” only after implementation and verification; announce dates when scope and delivery are sufficiently understood.
